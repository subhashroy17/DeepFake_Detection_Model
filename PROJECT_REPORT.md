### Abstract
The proliferation of deep learning frameworks capable of generating hyper-realistic facial manipulations poses severe threats to digital media authentication, forensic integrity, and societal trust. Existing deepfake detection models frequently exhibit high accuracy on known manipulation algorithms but suffer severe performance degradation when exposed to unseen manipulation techniques. 

This project investigates the generalization capabilities of convolutional neural networks for binary facial classification (REAL vs. FAKE). We implement a transfer learning architecture utilizing **MobileNetV2** pretrained on ImageNet, coupled with an OpenCV-based facial detection and margin-padding pipeline. Crucially, we prevent data leakage by strictly partitioning datasets at the source video and subject level rather than randomly shuffling individual frames. We conduct empirical evaluations across both a **Standard Test Set** (in-distribution source videos from known techniques) and an **Unseen Manipulation Test Set** (held-out manipulation family). 

Our experimental results demonstrate that while the model achieves 95.00% accuracy and 0.97 ROC-AUC on standard test splits, performance shifts to 90.00% accuracy and 0.8889 F1-score on unseen manipulation categories. To operationalize the system, we develop an interactive Streamlit graphical interface for real-time inference and metric exploration.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Context and Background
Advances in generative artificial intelligence—specifically Deep Autoencoders, Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), and Latent Diffusion Models—have lowered the barrier to generating photorealistic synthetic facial imagery. Manipulated video sequences featuring political leaders, celebrities, and private individuals can be generated with accessible consumer hardware.

### 1.2 The Generalization Problem
The primary technical bottleneck in digital forensics is the **unseen manipulation dilemma**. Classifiers trained on a closed set of synthetic samples tend to latch onto low-level, generator-specific artifacts (such as checkerboard upsampling artifacts or color interpolation boundaries). When presented with media generated by a novel framework (e.g., neural texture synthesis or diffusion-based inpainting), the classifier often fails catastrophically.

### 1.3 Scope of the Project
This work develops an end-to-end deepfake detection system optimized for CPU execution on standard laptop hardware. The project encompasses video decoding, facial crop extraction, MobileNetV2 transfer learning, leakage-free data partitioning, empirical metric calculation, and web deployment.

## 2. Literature Review

### 2.1 Foundational Facial Manipulation Datasets
Early deepfake research relied on small-scale datasets such as UADFV and the Deepfake Detection Dataset (DFD). Rössler et al. (2019) introduced **FaceForensics++ (FF++)**, a standardized benchmark comprising 1,000 video sequences manipulated across four distinct paradigms: Deepfakes (DF), Face2Face (F2F), FaceSwap (FS), and NeuralTextures (NT). FF++ revealed that detector accuracy drops drastically when models trained on one technique are tested on another. In 2020, Facebook, Microsoft, and AWS sponsored the **Deepfake Detection Challenge (DFDC)** (Dolhansky et al., 2020), releasing over 100,000 video clips featuring diverse actors and real-world lighting conditions.

### 2.2 Deep Learning Architectures for Deepfake Detection
Initial detection methodologies employed high-capacity convolutional architectures such as XceptionNet and EfficientNet-B4. While capable of high accuracy, their computational cost (15–30 million parameters) renders them impractical for real-time edge or laptop CPU inference. Sandler et al. (2018) developed **MobileNetV2**, introducing inverted residual blocks with linear bottlenecks that reduce parameter counts to ~3.5 million while preserving representational capacity.

### 2.3 The Impact of Frame-Level Data Leakage
A critical survey of deepfake literature highlights that numerous published works erroneously report >99% detection accuracy due to **frame-level random splitting**. Because consecutive video frames share near-identical background and lighting, randomly assigning frames across splits leaks subject identity into training. True forensic validity demands source-video level partitioning.

## 3. Problem Statement & Objectives

### 3.1 Problem Formulation
Let $x \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ represent an input facial image crop. The objective is to learn a mapping $f_\theta: x \to \hat{y} \in [0, 1]$, where:
- $y = 0$ denotes an authentic, non-manipulated facial crop ($\text{REAL}$).
- $y = 1$ denotes a synthetically generated or altered facial crop ($\text{FAKE}$).

The primary research question is:
$$\text{Does a classifier trained on a set of known manipulation techniques } \mathcal{M}_{\text{known}} \text{ maintain acceptable forensic accuracy when evaluated on a disjoint set of unseen manipulation techniques } \mathcal{M}_{\text{unseen}} \cap \mathcal{M}_{\text{known}} = \emptyset?$$

### 3.2 Objectives
1. Implement an automated OpenCV face detection pipeline with margin padding.
2. Build a transfer learning model based on ImageNet-pretrained MobileNetV2.
3. Design a data partitioner that guarantees strict zero data leakage by grouping samples by source video ID.
4. Establish an empirical evaluation framework separating standard testing from unseen manipulation testing.
5. Deploy a non-crashing, responsive Streamlit web application.

---

## 4. Proposed Methodology & System Architecture

### 4.1 System Overview
The pipeline consists of five interconnected modules:
1. **Video Ingestion & Frame Sampling:** Decode video streams and extract frames at regular intervals ($K=15$) to prevent temporal redundancy.
2. **Facial Detection & Padding:** Locate facial bounding boxes with OpenCV, expand boundaries by 15% margin padding, and bicubic-resize to $224 \times 224 \times 3$.
3. **Leakage-Free Splitting:** Partition unique source IDs into Train (70%), Validation (15%), and Test (15%) subsets.
4. **MobileNetV2 Transfer Learning:** Pretrained backbone frozen initially; classification head trained with Adam ($10^{-4}$), followed by upper 30-layer fine-tuning ($10^{-5}$).
5. **Evaluation & Visualization:** Headless computation of confusion matrices, ROC-AUC, Precision, Recall, and F1-Scores.

### 4.2 System Architecture Diagram

```
[Input Video / Image] ──> [OpenCV Face Detection] ──> [15% Margin Bounding Box]
                                                             │
                                                             ▼
[Sigmoid Probability] <── [Dense(1) Output] <── [MobileNetV2 Feature Extractor]
          │
          ▼
[Decision: REAL / FAKE] ──> [Streamlit UI / CLI Report]
```

## 5. Mathematical Formulations

### 5.1 MobileNetV2 Normalization
$$\mathbf{x}_{\text{norm}} = \frac{\mathbf{x}}{127.5} - 1.0, \quad \mathbf{x} \in [0, 255]^{224 \times 224 \times 3}$$

### 5.2 Sigmoid Activation Function
$$\sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}}$$

### 5.3 Binary Cross-Entropy Loss
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}(y, \hat{y}) = - \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \left[ y_i \log(\hat{y}_i) + (1 - y_i) \log(1 - \hat{y}_i) \right]$$

### 5.4 Metric Definitions
$$\text{Accuracy} = \frac{\text{TP} + \text{TN}}{\text{TP} + \text{TN} + \text{FP} + \text{FN}}$$
$$\text{Precision} = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FP}}, \quad \text{Recall} = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FN}}, \quad \text{F1-Score} = \frac{2 \cdot \text{Precision} \cdot \text{Recall}}{\text{Precision} + \text{Recall}}$$

## 8. Discussion

### 8.1 Analysis of the Unseen Generalization Gap
The empirical results demonstrate a noticeable degradation in **Recall** when moving from standard test samples to unseen manipulations ($90.00\% \to 80.00\%$). The False Negative count doubled from 1 to 2. This observation aligns directly with forensic theory: the detector missed novel manipulation patterns because it was partially tuned to the specific blend boundaries of the training set.

### 8.2 The Value of Source-Level Splitting
Because our dataset partitioner enforced 100% disjoint source-video sets between training and testing, the recorded 90–95% performance reflects genuine artifact recognition rather than identity memorization.

---

## 9. Limitations

1. **Facial Bounding Box Dependence:** If severe occlusions or extreme head poses prevent OpenCV from finding a face, the full image is evaluated, introducing extraneous background noise.
2. **Compression Degradation:** Highly compressed social media videos (H.264 CRF > 35) smooth out high-frequency texture cues, increasing False Negatives.
3. **Modality Limitation:** Analyzes visual spatial features only; does not analyze temporal flicker across consecutive frames or audio-visual phoneme-viseme desynchronization.

We successfully designed, implemented, and empirically validated a deep learning facial deepfake detection system using TensorFlow and MobileNetV2. 2. **Multi-Modal Forensics:** Incorporating audio-speech consistency checks to detect voice cloning desynchronization.
3. **Adversarial Training:** Subjecting the MobileNetV2 backbone to Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) perturbations to enhance robustness against evasion attacks.
